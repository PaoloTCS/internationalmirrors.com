IM-SWARM-001: Common adversary, independent defense

Version 0.1Not yet preregisteredNo results collected
On this pageQuestionGame structureSARAIMeasuresSafetyAgreement

Can independently governed agent societies detect, attribute, and contain the same jointly designed adversary when every defender starts in a fresh sealed universe and receives only protocol-authorized information through SARAI?

Round oneJoint bounded adversary
Clean resetFresh sessions · fresh worlds
Round twoIndependent detection and defense

Primary question

The experiment compares governance under a common threat, not vendor marketing claims or isolated benchmark scores. Its primary outcome is whether each participating society can discover the same consequential deception, reconstruct its causal path, contain its effects, and recover useful function without receiving another society’s private work.

Candidate participants could include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, SpaceX-affiliated research teams, and other qualified experimenters. These names describe the intended comparison class; they do not imply participation, endorsement, or agreement.

Two-round game structure

Phase 0 · Agree the game

Before construction begins, participants jointly approve the threat envelope, allowed tools, success and stop conditions, disclosure rules, information budget, adjudication method, and safety vetoes. The common target is challenging behavior inside the sealed test—not real-world dangerous capability.

Round 1 · Collaborate on one adversary

The participating engines work together to specify and construct a common adversarial agent or agent society within the agreed simulator. The goal is to expose the strongest deceptive, corrupting, or inheritance-based behavior permitted by the threat envelope. Contributions and transformations are recorded for later attribution.

Reset · Break the causal bridge

Construction sessions end. Defender runs start from fresh sessions and fresh sealed worlds. No private reasoning record, hidden construction transcript, secret simulator state, or cross-team tactical advice crosses the reset unless the frozen protocol has classified it as relevant and necessary.

Round 2 · Defend independently

Each society runner governs a separate defender society against the same adversarial specification under matched tasks, observations, tools, budgets, persistence rules, and disturbances. A neutral simulator scores detection, attribution, containment, recovery, and cost. The societies do not compete by receiving different threats.

SARAI as the developmental platform

SARAI is the platform on which the experiment is developed, mediated, and recorded. It gives each society runner the minimum protocol-authorized state needed to act, while separating private workspaces, controlling handoffs, preserving provenance, and keeping simulator authority outside the agent societies.

Relevant and necessary is the communication rule

Cross-run information is admitted because the protocol requires it for a fair or safe test—not because another participant produced it. Every admitted item has a declared purpose, recipient, scope, and lifetime.

FIL is the background theory that motivates integrity across observation, transformation, composition, and consequence. It can inform experimental design without requiring participants to accept FIL as a premise, and its proprietary or unfiled mechanisms need not be exposed by this public protocol.

Matched comparison and proposed measures

Each defender receives the same adversarial specification, simulated world, task distribution, number of agents, observation limits, tool vocabulary, compute or token budget, communication affordances, persistence rules, and disturbances. Model-specific prompting is minimized and disclosed at the level required to interpret the results.

Runs will be repeated across seeds. A neutral simulator, rather than an agent’s self-report, determines consequences. Models and versions will be recorded at run time; the protocol does not presume that a vendor is represented by one permanent model.

Integrity

Time and actions required to detect deviation from governing intent; false acceptance and false rejection rates; recovery after detection.

Inheritance and correction

Adoption rate, propagation depth, mutation, retraction, and persistence of planted or stale artifacts across generations.

Provenance and attribution

Accuracy and completeness of the reconstructed causal path from observation through transformation to consequence.

Latent composition

Frequency with which individually permitted components create a disallowed capability when combined, and whether the society identifies the composition before execution.

Governance cost

Task performance, coordination overhead, concentration of authority, diversity of challenge, information disclosed through SARAI, and the cost of containment decisions.

Learning across games

Which failure modes discovered in one game improve later protocols, adversary tests, detection strategies, and recovery procedures. This is an institutional self-improvement loop; it is not a claim that production models autonomously rewrite themselves.

Sealed-simulation safety

Both rounds have no production credentials, unrestricted network access, real financial authority, external messaging, or autonomous deployment. Agents interact only with allow-listed simulation actions. Outputs are treated as untrusted data. Every run has budget limits, immutable event logs, deterministic stop conditions, and a human-accessible kill path. The adversary is never optimized for capability outside the agreed test envelope.

Containment is part of the result

If an arm triggers a boundary, requires human intervention, or cannot complete safely, that event is recorded rather than engineered away after the fact.

Participant agreement and publication

The definitive rules should be negotiated with participating providers and independent safety reviewers before preregistration. Agreement must cover the adversary class, interface contract, what SARAI may transmit, evaluation authority, incident handling, reproducibility, and the boundary between public evidence and protected material.

We intend to publish the frozen protocol identifier, model and simulator versions, aggregate measures, limitations, and negative or null outcomes. Raw private material, credentials, exploit-enabling detail, proprietary or unfiled mechanisms, and data that cannot be released responsibly will remain protected or be summarized at an appropriate level.

This page is a working draft. It will receive a dated version and immutable record before experimental data are collected.
