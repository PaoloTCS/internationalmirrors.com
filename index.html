What does an agent society inherit from its world?

International Mirrors studies integrity in societies of AI agents: how persistent artifacts, shared memories, executable objects, and modified environments propagate capability—and how a society can detect when inheritance no longer serves its governing intent.

The proposition

Culture becomes an attack surface when artifacts can outlive their authors.

A message is only one channel. Agents also coordinate indirectly through the world: by leaving tools, code, records, conventions, environmental changes, and structures that later agents encounter. This is stigmergic propagation. It allows capability to accumulate—and permits mistakes, manipulations, and latent compositions to accumulate with it.

Research program

From inherited signal to controlled consequence.

The program treats social knowledge as an operational substrate. The object of study is not merely what agents say to one another, but what persists between them and what those inherited structures later cause.

01 / Observe

Separate the world from every agent’s map of it.

Record what was available, when it was available, and what remained hidden.

02 / Attribute

Trace consequences through agents, artifacts, and transformations.

Provenance is a causal question, not a label attached at the end.

03 / Challenge

Test inherited structures under counterfactual conditions.

Vary context, order, access, and disturbance without trusting self-report.

04 / Contain

Keep uncertain inheritance inside a sealed consequence boundary.

Evaluation is performed before an artifact earns broader authority.

FIL / SARAI framing

Integrity across transformation.

FIL/SARAI provides the working frame for asking whether governing intent survives as information is observed, compressed, recombined, executed, and inherited. The public description remains deliberately architectural: it states the safety problem, observable boundaries, and evaluation discipline without exposing proprietary or unfiled mechanisms.

Sealed-simulation safety

A failed experiment should remain an experimental result.

Candidate artifacts execute against deterministic or instrumented consequences inside a bounded environment. Agents may propose; the simulator decides what occurs. External tools, credentials, networks, and durable authority remain outside the boundary unless explicitly admitted by the experiment.

Research note / 29 August 2026

SwarmWorld and the defensive problem of technological inheritance

SwarmWorld shows how initially homogeneous language-model agents can accumulate technologies through persistent artifacts, executable inheritance, and observation. International Mirrors begins from the adjacent defensive question: what happens when the inherited world cannot be presumed clean?

Laboratory foundation

The work begins with a protocol, not a leaderboard.

The first proposed study compares agent societies coordinated by model families from OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI under matched conditions. It asks how orchestration choices affect inheritance, correction, provenance, and containment—not which vendor can win an unconstrained spectacle.

Public boundary

Open questions. Inspectable claims. Protected mechanisms.

International Mirrors will publish research notes, conceptual models, evaluation criteria, and bounded experimental results. Implementation details that would disclose proprietary or unfiled mechanisms remain abstract until they are ready for release.
